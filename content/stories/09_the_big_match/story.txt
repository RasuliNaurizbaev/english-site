The Big Match

Leo is fifteen and plays football for his school team. He practises three times a week after lessons, and on Saturdays the team plays matches against other schools. This year, his team did very well, and now they were in the final of the city competition.

The final was on Saturday at the city stadium. All week, Leo couldn't think about anything else. In class, he didn't listen to the teacher, and at night he dreamed about scoring goals.

On Friday, the coach, Mr Wilson, called the team together. "Tomorrow will be difficult," he said. "The other team is strong and fast. But if you work together as a team, you can win. Go to bed early, eat a good breakfast and don't be late."

On Saturday, the stadium was full. Leo's parents and his little brother sat in the crowd with a big sign: "GO, LEO!" When the players came out, everybody shouted and clapped. Leo's legs felt heavy.

The first half was terrible for Leo's team. After ten minutes, the other team scored a goal. Then, just before half-time, they scored again. In the break, the players were sad and angry. Some of them shouted at each other.

Mr Wilson stayed calm. "Listen," he said. "Shouting doesn't help. Two goals is not the end of the world. Stop playing alone and pass the ball. Believe in yourselves."

In the second half, the team played much better. They passed the ball and helped each other. After sixty minutes, Leo's best friend Omar scored. The crowd went crazy. Ten minutes later, Leo kicked the ball from the corner, and Omar hit it with his head. Two all!

The last minutes were very exciting. Everyone in the stadium was standing. Then, in the ninetieth minute, Leo got the ball. He ran past one player, then another. He was alone in front of the goal. He kicked, and the ball flew into the net!

A few seconds later, the game was over. Leo's team won three to two. The players jumped and hugged each other, and some of them cried with happiness. Leo's parents ran onto the field, and his little brother jumped into his arms.

Mr Wilson shook Leo's hand. "Well done," he said. "But remember, you didn't win because of one goal. You won because you didn't give up."

That evening, the whole team had pizza at a local restaurant. They looked at the cup on the table and laughed about the terrible first half. Leo knew that he would remember this day for the rest of his life.
