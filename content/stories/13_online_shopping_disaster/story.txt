The Online Shopping Disaster

Kate Wilson hated going to the shops. The shopping centre was always crowded, the music was too loud and she could never find anything in her size. So when her best friend, Emma, invited her to her wedding in June, Kate decided to buy everything online.

It was a Sunday evening, three weeks before the wedding. Kate sat on the sofa with her laptop and a cup of tea. After an hour of searching, she found the perfect dress. It was light green, long and elegant, and it was on sale. She also chose a pair of silver shoes and a small bag. "Easy," she said to her cat, Pepper. "Why didn't I always do this?"

She put her name, address and card details into the website and clicked "Buy now". A message appeared on the screen: "Thank you for your order! Your parcel will be delivered within five working days." Kate smiled and went to bed happy.

On Thursday, a parcel arrived. Kate opened it excitedly. Inside she found the silver shoes and the bag, but not the dress. Instead, there was a bright orange T-shirt with a picture of a cartoon duck on it. It was size XXL. Kate held it up in front of the mirror. It was as long as a dress, but it was definitely not a wedding dress.

She looked at the shoes. At least they were right, she thought. Then she tried them on. They were two sizes too small. She checked her order on the website and saw the problem. She had chosen size 37, but she had wanted 39. It was her own mistake.

Kate took a deep breath. She decided to contact customer service. On the website there was a chat window. A message appeared: "Hi, I'm Lily. How can I help you today?"

Kate explained everything: the orange T-shirt, the missing dress and the shoes that were too small. She was polite, but she was worried. "The wedding is in two and a half weeks," she wrote. "I really need that dress."

"I'm very sorry," Lily answered. "It seems that your dress was sent to another customer by mistake. We will send you a new one by express delivery. It should arrive on Tuesday. You can also send the shoes back for free, and we'll send you a bigger size."

Kate felt much better. She packed the T-shirt and the shoes into the box, took it to the post office and sent it back. "Everything will be fine," she told Pepper. The cat didn't look so sure.

On Tuesday, Kate stayed at home all day. She worked on her laptop at the kitchen table and listened for the doorbell. Nobody came. At six o'clock in the evening, she got an email. "Good news! Your parcel has been delivered." Kate ran to the door and opened it. There was nothing there. She looked behind the plants, under the doormat and even in the rubbish bin. No parcel.

She went back to the chat. This time a man called Tom answered. He checked the information and found a photo that the driver had taken. It showed a blue door with the number 18 on it. "That's not my door," Kate wrote. "I live at number 81!" Tom said that the driver had probably read the number the wrong way. He promised to contact the delivery company, but he said it could take a few days.

Kate didn't want to wait a few days. She put on her coat and walked down Green Street. Number 18 was at the other end, about ten minutes from her house. She knocked on the blue door, but nobody answered. She wrote a short note with her name and phone number, put it through the letterbox and went home. She felt tired and a little hopeless. If the dress didn't arrive, she would have to go to the crowded shopping centre after all.

That night, at about ten o'clock, her phone rang. It was a number she didn't know.

"Hello, is that Kate?" said a friendly voice. "My name is George. I live at number 18. I've just got home from work and found your note. I think I have something of yours. A big box with your name on it was in front of my door."

Kate almost dropped the phone. "Oh, thank you! I can come and get it now," she said.

"Don't worry, it's late," George said. "I take my dog for a walk every evening. I'll bring it to you in ten minutes."

Ten minutes later, there was a knock at the door. A tall man in his sixties was standing there with a big black dog and the box under his arm. "Here you are," he said with a smile. "I hope it's something nice."

Kate opened the box right there on the doorstep. Inside was the light green dress. It was exactly the right size. "It's for my best friend's wedding," she explained. "You've saved me!"

George laughed. "My wife and I got married forty years ago," he said. "She made her own dress. Things were simpler then, I think."

Kate invited him in for a cup of tea, and they talked for half an hour. George told her that the same thing had happened to him last year. His new boots had been delivered to number 81, but the woman who lived there before Kate had never returned them. "So you see," he said, "the street owes me a pair of boots."

The new shoes, in the right size, arrived on Friday, at the right address. On the day of the wedding, Kate looked beautiful in her green dress and silver shoes. Emma loved it. "Where did you buy it?" she asked.

"Online," Kate said. "But it's a long story."

The next week, Kate went to a shoe shop in the shopping centre. She tried on ten pairs of boots and finally bought a warm brown pair in a man's size 44. Then she walked down Green Street to number 18 and left a box in front of the blue door. On top, there was a note: "From number 81. Better late than never. Thank you, George!"
