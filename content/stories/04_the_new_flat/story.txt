The New Flat

Last spring, Peter and his wife Emma decided to move. Their old flat was small and dark, and it was in a noisy street near the motorway. They wanted a bigger, quieter place with a garden for their dog, Max.

For two months they looked for a new home. They visited a lot of flats and houses, but some were too expensive, some were too far from the city centre, and some were dirty and old. Emma was almost ready to give up. Then one day Peter found a flat on the internet: two bedrooms on the ground floor, with a small garden, in a quiet neighbourhood near a park. The rent wasn't cheap, but it was OK for them.

They met the landlord, an elderly man called Mr Brown. He showed them around. The living room was large and bright, with big windows. The kitchen had a new cooker, a fridge, a dishwasher and a washing machine. Emma loved it at once. They signed the papers the same day.

Moving day was hard work. Peter's brother came with a big car to help them. They packed everything into boxes: books, plates, cups, clothes and toys for Max. The heaviest thing was the sofa. It was too wide for the door, so they had to turn it on its side. Peter hurt his back a little, but they finally got it inside.

In the evening, the new flat was full of boxes, and everything was a mess. They were too tired to cook, so they ordered a pizza and ate it on the floor of the living room. Max ran around the garden happily.

The next day there was a knock at the door. It was their neighbour, a friendly woman called Mrs Taylor. She brought them a cake. "Welcome to the neighbourhood!" she said. "If you need anything, just ask." She told them about the local shops, the bus stop and the best place to buy fresh bread.

During the next few weeks, Peter and Emma slowly made the flat their home. They painted the bedroom walls light green, bought new curtains and a comfortable armchair, and hung pictures on the walls. Emma planted flowers and tomatoes in the garden. Peter built a small wooden house for Max.

One warm Saturday in June, they invited their family and friends to a party. Everyone sat in the garden, ate, talked and laughed until late. "Your new home is lovely," said Peter's mother.

Peter looked at Emma and smiled. "Yes," he said. "It was hard work, but now it's perfect."
