The Lost Dog

The Parker family lived in a small house at the edge of a quiet town. There were four of them: Mr and Mrs Parker, their daughter Lucy, who was twelve, and her little brother Ben, who was eight. But if you asked Lucy, she always said there were five. The fifth member of the family was Max, a big brown dog with soft ears and a very long tail.

The Parkers had found Max three years earlier. He had been a tiny puppy then, and he had been sitting alone in a box outside a supermarket. Lucy had cried until her parents agreed to take him home. Since that day, Max had followed her everywhere. He used to wait for her at the gate every afternoon when she came back from school.

One evening in October, the weather changed very quickly. The sky became dark, and a strong wind started to blow. By eight o'clock there was a terrible storm. The rain was hitting the windows, and the trees in the garden were bending in the wind. Then there was a loud crash of thunder, the loudest the children had ever heard.

Max hated thunder. When the crash came, he jumped up and ran to the back door. At that moment Mr Parker was coming in from the garden with some wood for the fire. The door was open for only a few seconds, but it was enough. Max ran past him into the dark and disappeared.

"Max! Max, come back!" shouted Mr Parker. He ran outside, but he couldn't see anything in the heavy rain. He looked for twenty minutes and came back wet and worried. "I'm sorry," he said quietly. "I couldn't find him."

Lucy didn't sleep that night. She lay in bed and listened to the storm. "If he is cold and lost, he will be so frightened," she thought. In the morning the storm had stopped, but Max had not come home.

The family started to search immediately. Mrs Parker called the local vet and the police station. Mr Parker drove slowly along every street in the town. Lucy and Ben walked through the park and the fields behind the school, calling Max's name again and again. Nobody had seen him.

In the afternoon, Lucy had an idea. "Let's make posters," she said. She found a nice photo of Max on her mother's phone, and they printed fifty copies. Under the photo, in big letters, they wrote: "LOST DOG. His name is Max. He is brown, big and very friendly. Please call us!" They added their phone number. Then the whole family went out and put the posters on trees, shop windows and bus stops.

Mrs Parker also posted the photo on social media. She wrote a short message and asked people to share it. By the evening, the post had been shared more than three hundred times. Many people wrote kind comments. "I hope you find him soon," wrote one woman. "We will look in our garden," wrote another.

The phone started ringing the next day. A man said that he had seen a brown dog near the train station. The Parkers drove there at once, but it was a different dog, a small one with a red collar. Later, a girl called and told them that a big dog was sleeping behind the sports centre. Again, it wasn't Max. Each time, Lucy's hope grew a little, and each time it broke again.

Three days passed, then four. Ben stopped asking questions at dinner. Lucy kept Max's old blue ball in her bag and held it when she felt sad. Her teacher noticed that she was quiet at school. "Don't give up," the teacher said. "Dogs are clever. They often find their way home."

On the fifth day, Mrs Parker received a message from a woman called Mrs Holmes. She lived on a farm about ten kilometres outside the town. "I think I have seen your dog," she wrote. "There is a brown dog with my sheep. He has been here for two days. He won't come near me, but he is not hurting the animals. He seems to be guarding them."

The family got into the car immediately. Nobody spoke during the journey. Lucy was holding the blue ball so tightly that her fingers hurt. "What if it's not him again?" Ben whispered. "Then we will keep looking," said his father.

The farm was on a green hill, and the sheep were walking slowly in a large field. At first Lucy saw only white sheep. Then, in the middle of them, she saw something brown. It was lying in the grass with its head up, watching the sheep carefully.

Lucy opened the gate and shouted as loudly as she could. "Max!"

The brown dog stood up. For a second it didn't move. Then its long tail started to wave, and it ran across the field so fast that the sheep jumped out of its way. It was Max. He jumped on Lucy and knocked her down into the wet grass, and he licked her face again and again. Lucy was laughing and crying at the same time. Ben threw his arms around Max's neck, and even Mr Parker had tears in his eyes.

Mrs Holmes came out of the house and smiled. "So he really is yours," she said. "He's a very good dog. I have never seen a dog look after sheep like that. If you ever want to sell him, I'll buy him!"

"Sorry," laughed Lucy. "He's not for sale."

Nobody knew exactly how Max had got to the farm. Mr Parker thought that the storm had frightened him so much that he had run for hours without stopping. "Maybe he felt safe with the sheep," said Mrs Parker.

When they got home, Mrs Parker wrote a new post on social media. "Max is home! Thank you to everyone who helped us." Then she put a new collar on Max with a small metal tag. It had his name and their phone number on it.

That night there was a little rain, but no thunder. Max slept on the floor next to Lucy's bed, with his old blue ball between his paws. Lucy looked at him and smiled. "Next time there's a storm," she said, "we'll stay inside together."
