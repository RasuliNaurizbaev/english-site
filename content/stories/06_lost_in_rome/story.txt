Lost in Rome

Last summer, Daniel travelled abroad alone for the first time. He went to Rome for five days. He was excited, but also a little worried, because he didn't speak Italian.

On the third day, he decided to visit a famous old church on the other side of the city. In the morning, he left his hotel with a map and his phone. He wanted to walk, because the weather was lovely and he wanted to see the narrow streets and small squares.

For the first hour, everything was fine. He stopped at a market and bought some fruit, then he took photos of an ancient building. But soon the streets became more and more confusing. They all looked the same. Daniel looked at his map, but he couldn't find the name of the street. Then he took out his phone, but the battery was empty! He forgot to charge it the night before.

Daniel felt afraid. He was lost in a big foreign city, he didn't know the language, and his phone didn't work. He walked for another twenty minutes, but he only got more lost. It was hot, and he was hungry and thirsty.

Finally, he saw a small café on a corner. An old man was sitting at a table outside, reading a newspaper. Daniel went up to him. "Excuse me," he said slowly. "Do you speak English? I'm lost."

The man smiled. "A little," he answered. "Where do you want to go?"

Daniel showed him the name of the church on the map. The man laughed. "Oh, it's very near! But first, sit down. You look tired." He ordered two coffees and a glass of water for Daniel. They talked for half an hour. The man's name was Marco. He was a retired teacher, and his son lived in London. He told Daniel about the history of the area and gave him the name of a good local restaurant.

Then Marco walked with him to the church. It was only ten minutes away. "Here you are," he said. "Enjoy Rome!" Daniel thanked him again and again.

The church was amazing, but Daniel decided that the best part of his day was not the sightseeing. It was the conversation with Marco. That evening, he had dinner at Marco's restaurant. The pasta was delicious.

When he got back home, Daniel sent Marco a card. "Thank you for your help," he wrote. "Next time I come to Rome, I'll buy the coffee!"

Now, before he goes out in a new city, Daniel always remembers two things: charge your phone, and don't be afraid to ask for help.
