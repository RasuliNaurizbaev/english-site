The Job Interview

Sara was twenty-seven, and for three years she had worked in a busy shop in the town centre. She liked her colleagues, but the job was boring and the pay was low. In the evenings she studied marketing online. She had always dreamed of working in an office with creative people, where her ideas were important.

One Monday morning, she was drinking coffee in the kitchen when her phone rang. It was a woman called Mrs Taylor from a travel company. "We have read your application," she said. "We would like to invite you to an interview on Friday at ten o'clock." Sara could hardly believe it. She had sent more than twenty applications, and this was the first answer.

"Thank you so much," Sara said. "I'll be there." When she put the phone down, she jumped up and down in the kitchen. Then she suddenly stopped. She had never had a real job interview before. What would they ask? What should she wear? What if she said something stupid?

That evening she called her older brother, Daniel. He worked in a bank and had been to many interviews. "Don't worry," he told her. "Prepare well and be yourself. If you know a lot about the company, they will see that you are interested." He advised her to read the company's website and to think about her strengths and weaknesses.

For the next four days, Sara prepared every evening after work. She read everything about the company. She learned that it was started fifteen years ago by two friends, and that it organised trips for families and young people. She wrote down possible questions and practised her answers in front of the mirror. Her flatmate, Lucy, pretended to be the manager. "Why do you want this job?" Lucy asked in a deep, serious voice, and they both laughed.

On Thursday night, Sara couldn't sleep. She kept thinking about the interview. At two o'clock in the morning she was still awake, looking at the ceiling. She finally fell asleep at four, and she forgot to set her alarm.

When she woke up, it was already half past eight. "Oh no!" she shouted. She jumped out of bed, put on the dark blue dress she had chosen the day before and ran out of the flat without breakfast. The bus was full and slow because of the traffic. Sara looked at her watch every minute. Her heart was beating fast.

She arrived at the office at five to ten, out of breath, but on time. The receptionist asked her to wait. Sara sat down and tried to breathe slowly. Then she noticed something terrible. In her hurry, she had left her folder at home. Her CV, her notes and all her answers were on the kitchen table.

At that moment, a tall man with glasses opened the door. "Sara Collins? I'm Mr Evans, the manager. Please come in." In the room there was also Mrs Taylor, the woman from the phone call. They both smiled and asked her to sit down.

The first questions were easy. They asked about her studies and her job in the shop. Sara answered clearly, and she started to feel a little better. Then Mr Evans asked, "Tell me, what do you know about our company?"

Sara opened her mouth, but suddenly her mind was empty. She had read so much, but now she couldn't remember anything. She said the company was twenty years old. Mrs Taylor looked at her notes. "Actually, it's fifteen," she said politely. Sara felt her face go red. She wanted to disappear.

For a few seconds nobody spoke. Sara thought about leaving. Then she remembered Daniel's words: "Be yourself." She took a deep breath.

"I'm sorry," she said. "To be honest, I'm very nervous. I didn't sleep last night, and this morning I left all my notes at home. I prepared a lot, but right now my head is a mess. Can I tell you why I really want this job instead?"

Mr Evans looked surprised, but he nodded. "Please do."

So Sara told them the truth. She explained that she had never travelled much as a child because her family didn't have much money. Her first trip abroad was to Spain when she was nineteen, and it had changed her life. "I saw that travel isn't only for rich people," she said. "If I worked here, I would like to help more young people take their first trip. I have some ideas for cheap student holidays." She described two of her ideas. As she spoke, she forgot about being nervous. Her voice became stronger, and her eyes were shining.

When she finished, Mrs Taylor was smiling. Mr Evans wrote something in his notebook. "Thank you, Sara," he said. "We will call you next week."

On the bus home, Sara felt sure that she had failed. "I said the wrong year, and I told them I was a mess," she said to Lucy that evening. "They will never choose me." Lucy made her a cup of tea and told her to stop worrying.

Six days later, Mrs Taylor called. Sara's hands were shaking when she answered. "We have interviewed eight people," Mrs Taylor said. "Many of them knew every fact about our company. But you were the only one who was honest with us, and your ideas were excellent. We would like to offer you the job."

Sara was silent for a moment. Then she said, "Yes! Thank you! Yes!"

A month later, Sara started her new job. On her first day, Mr Evans showed her to her desk. "You know," he said, "when you told us the truth, I knew you were the right person. Anyone can learn facts. It's much harder to learn honesty."

Sara smiled. Since that day, she has always kept two things in her bag: a copy of her notes and a second alarm clock.
