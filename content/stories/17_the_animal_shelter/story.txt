The Animal Shelter

Jack Wilson was fifteen, and he didn't like animals very much. He didn't hate them, but he thought they were dirty, noisy and boring. He preferred computer games, football and sleeping late on Saturdays. So when his teacher, Mr Hughes, explained the new school project, Jack was not happy at all.

"This term, every student will do ten hours of volunteer work," said Mr Hughes. "You can choose a library, a hospital, a sports club or a charity. At the end, you will write a report and give a short presentation."

Most of Jack's friends quickly chose the sports club. Jack waited too long, and when he looked at the list again, there was only one place left: the Green Hill Animal Shelter. "Great," he said to himself. "Ten hours with dogs and cats."

On his first Saturday, his mum drove him to the shelter. It was an old building with a big garden behind it. As soon as he opened the door, he heard dogs barking. A woman in a green jacket came to meet him. She had short dark hair and a friendly face. "Hi, you must be Jack. I'm Sarah, the manager," she said. "Have you ever worked with animals before?"

"No," said Jack. "And I'm not really an animal person."

Sarah only smiled. "That's fine. Most people say that on their first day."

She gave him a pair of gloves and a bucket. His first job was to clean the dog kennels. It was hard, smelly work, and after an hour his back hurt. Then he had to wash the food bowls and carry heavy bags of dog food from the car to the storeroom. When his mum picked him up, he said, "I've done two hours. Only eight more to go."

The next Saturday, Sarah asked him to help with walking the dogs. Most of them were excited and pulled on their leads. But one dog stayed at the back of his kennel and didn't come out. He was a thin grey dog with sad eyes and a scar on his nose.

"That's Bruno," said Sarah. "He was found on the street two months ago. Someone had left him tied to a tree in the middle of winter. He doesn't trust people now. Nobody has wanted to take him home."

Jack looked at Bruno for a long time. He felt something strange inside, something he hadn't expected. "Can I try?" he asked.

Sarah gave him a few dog biscuits. "Don't go in. Just sit near the door and talk to him quietly. Let him come to you."

So Jack sat on the cold floor and talked. He felt a bit silly at first. He told Bruno about school, about his favourite football team and about his annoying little sister. Bruno didn't move. After twenty minutes, Jack put a biscuit on the floor and went away.

From then on, Jack spent part of every visit sitting outside Bruno's kennel. On the third week, Bruno ate the biscuit while Jack was still there. On the fourth week, he came close enough to smell Jack's hand. On the fifth week, he let Jack touch his head. Jack was so happy that he took a photo and sent it to his mum.

Something else was changing, too. Jack started to notice how hard Sarah and the other volunteers worked. They came in early, stayed late and never complained. He learned how to give medicine to a cat, how to brush a dog and how to check if an animal was ill. He even started reading about animals on the internet at home. His parents were surprised. "You used to say animals were boring," his dad said one evening. Jack shrugged. "I was wrong," he said.

By the time he had finished his ten hours, Jack didn't want to stop. He kept going every Saturday.

One day he found Sarah in her office, looking at some papers with a worried face. "Is everything OK?" he asked.

Sarah sighed. "Not really. The roof is leaking, and we need a new one before winter. It will cost about three thousand pounds. We don't have that money. If we can't repair it, some kennels will have to close."

Jack thought about it all the way home. That evening he called his friends Emma and Tom. "I have an idea," he said. "What if we organise a fundraising event at school?"

The next few weeks were very busy. Jack and his friends talked to Mr Hughes, and the head teacher agreed to let them use the school hall. They decided to have a cake sale, a raffle and a "Dog Show" where people could bring their own dogs. Emma designed colourful posters. Tom asked local shops to give prizes for the raffle. Jack made a short video about the shelter, and Bruno was the star of it.

The video was shared on the school website and on social media. Jack was nervous. "What if nobody comes?" he asked Sarah. "Then we'll eat a lot of cake," she said, laughing.

But on the day of the event, the hall was full. Families came with children, grandparents and dogs of every size. There was a tiny white dog in a pink jumper and a huge black dog who won the prize for "Best Smile". People bought cakes, raffle tickets and T-shirts with the shelter's name on them.

At the end of the day, Jack and Sarah counted the money together. Jack's hands were shaking. "Three thousand four hundred and twenty pounds," Sarah said slowly. Then she hugged him. "Jack, you've saved the shelter."

But the best moment was still to come. While they were packing up, a young couple came to Jack. "We saw your video," the woman said. "We would like to meet Bruno. We have a big garden and a lot of time. Do you think he would like us?"

Two weeks later, Bruno went to his new home. Jack was happy for him, but he also felt a little sad. On the last day, Bruno put his head on Jack's knee and looked at him with his grey eyes.

When Jack gave his presentation at school, he finished with these words: "I started this project because I had to. I thought I didn't like animals. Now I know that I just didn't know them." Then he smiled. "And I'm still volunteering every Saturday."
