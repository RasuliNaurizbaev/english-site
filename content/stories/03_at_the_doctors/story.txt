At the Doctor's

Maria is a student. She studies biology at university and works part-time in a bookshop. Last week she didn't feel well. On Monday she had a headache, and on Tuesday she started to cough. She thought it was just a cold, so she didn't do anything about it.

On Wednesday morning, she woke up with a sore throat and a high temperature. Her whole body hurt, and she felt very tired. She couldn't go to her lessons, so she called the bookshop and told her boss that she was ill. "Stay at home and rest," he said. "Your health is more important."

Her flatmate, Anna, was worried. "You look terrible," she said. "You should see a doctor." Maria didn't want to go, but Anna called the clinic and booked an appointment for eleven o'clock.

The clinic was not far, but Maria felt too weak to walk, so they took a taxi. In the waiting room there were a lot of people: an old man, a mother with a crying baby and a young man with a broken arm. They waited for forty minutes. Finally, a nurse called Maria's name.

The doctor was a tall man with glasses. "What's wrong?" he asked. Maria told him about her headache, her cough and her sore throat. The doctor checked her temperature. It was thirty-nine degrees. Then he looked at her throat and listened to her heart.

"You've got the flu," he said. "It's not dangerous, but you must stay in bed for a few days. Drink a lot of water and hot tea with honey and lemon." He wrote the name of some medicine on a piece of paper. "Take one pill three times a day, after meals. And don't go to work or university until you feel better."

On the way home, they stopped at a pharmacy and bought the medicine. Anna also went to the supermarket and bought fruit, vegetables and a chicken. In the evening she cooked a big pan of chicken soup. "My grandmother always made this soup when I was ill," she said. "It's the best medicine!"

For the next three days, Maria slept a lot, read a novel and watched films on her laptop. She took her pills and drank lots of tea. Her friends sent her messages and funny videos. By Sunday her temperature was normal, and she felt much better.

On Monday she went back to university. "Thank you for looking after me," she said to Anna. "You're a wonderful friend."

Anna smiled. "No problem. But next time, go to the doctor earlier!"
