The Storm in the Mountains

Every summer, the Miller family goes on holiday together. Usually they stay in a hotel by the sea, but this year sixteen-year-old Emily had a different idea. "Let's go camping in the mountains," she said. "No internet, no TV, just nature." Her father liked the idea at once. Her mother was not so sure, and her twelve-year-old brother Josh was angry. "No Wi-Fi for a whole week? That's terrible!"

But in the end, Emily won. In July, they packed the car with a tent, warm sweaters, boots, food, a small cooker and a box of medicine. They drove for six hours to a national park in the north. The road went higher and higher, past lakes, forests and waterfalls. Even Josh put his phone away and looked out of the window. "Wow," he said quietly. "It's beautiful."

The campsite was next to a clear blue lake. There were only a few other tents, and the air was fresh and cool. Their father and Josh put up the tent, while Emily and her mother collected wood for a fire. In the evening, they cooked sausages and potatoes on the fire and watched the sun go down behind the mountains. When it got dark, they saw thousands of stars. In the city, you can never see so many.

The first few days were wonderful. They went hiking along forest paths, swam in the cold lake and had picnics on the hills. Their father taught Josh how to fish, and on the third day Josh caught a big fish. He was so proud that he took twenty photos of it, with his phone, of course! Emily drew pictures of flowers and birds in her notebook, and their mother read books in the sunshine. Nobody missed the internet. Well, almost nobody.

On the fifth day, they decided to climb to the top of the highest mountain in the park. A man who worked in the park gave them some advice. "The weather can change very quickly up there," he said. "Take warm clothes and rain jackets, and come back before three o'clock."

They started early in the morning. The path was narrow and difficult, and they had to stop often to rest. But the views were amazing. After four hours, they finally reached the top. They could see for many kilometres in every direction: green valleys, dark forests and lakes that looked like small mirrors. They took photos, ate their sandwiches and felt very happy.

But then Emily saw something. In the west, the sky was getting dark. Big black clouds were moving towards them very fast. "Dad," she said, "I think a storm is coming."

They started down the mountain quickly, but after twenty minutes the storm arrived. First the wind became very strong, then heavy rain started to fall. There was thunder and lightning all around them. The path turned into a river of water, and it was difficult to see anything. Josh fell and hurt his knee. He sat on a rock and started to cry.

Their father stayed calm. "We can't continue in this weather," he said. "It's too dangerous. We need to find somewhere safe." Then Emily remembered something. On the way up, she saw a small wooden house next to the path. "There's a little house near here!" she shouted. "Follow me!"

Five minutes later, they found it. It was old and empty, but it was dry inside. They took off their wet jackets and sat close together. Their mother looked at Josh's knee. It wasn't broken, but it was very sore. She washed it with water from her bottle and put some medicine on it.

They waited in the little house for two hours. They were cold and tired, but nobody complained. They ate the rest of their chocolate and told funny stories. Josh even laughed when their father sang terrible old songs to make everyone feel better.

At last, the storm stopped. The sun came out, and there was a huge rainbow over the valley. Slowly and carefully, they walked down to the campsite. Josh's knee hurt, but he walked all the way without crying.

That night, they sat by the fire again. Everyone was very quiet. "You know," Josh said finally, "today was scary. But it was also the best adventure of my life. I'm glad we didn't stay in a hotel."

Emily smiled. "So you don't miss the Wi-Fi?"

Josh thought for a moment. "Well... maybe a little," he said. Everyone laughed.

The next year, the Millers went camping again. And this time, Josh was the first to say, "Let's go to the mountains!"
